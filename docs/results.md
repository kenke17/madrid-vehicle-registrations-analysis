# Results

This document summarises the main results of the Bachelor's Thesis on new passenger car registrations in the Community of Madrid between January 2015 and December 2025.

## 1. Final analytical dataset

After importing, cleaning and filtering the original monthly DGT records, the final master dataset contains:

- **3,115,063 valid new passenger car registrations**
- **132 monthly periods**
- **11 complete calendar years**
- Registrations associated with municipalities in the Community of Madrid

The study analyses registrations rather than the complete stock of vehicles currently in circulation.

## 2. Annual evolution

Passenger car registrations increased substantially over the study period, although the trend was interrupted in 2020.

| Year | Registrations | Share of total |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2015 | 224,111 | 7.19% |
| 2016 | 239,538 | 7.69% |
| 2017 | 264,654 | 8.50% |
| 2018 | 310,610 | 9.97% |
| 2019 | 313,546 | 10.07% |
| 2020 | 256,949 | 8.25% |
| 2021 | 255,448 | 8.20% |
| 2022 | 270,732 | 8.69% |
| 2023 | 310,958 | 9.98% |
| 2024 | 316,880 | 10.17% |
| 2025 | 351,637 | 11.29% |

Registrations increased from **224,111 in 2015** to **351,637 in 2025**.

The highest annual volume of the study period was recorded in 2025.

## 3. Disruption in 2020 and subsequent recovery

Registrations fell from 313,546 in 2019 to 256,949 in 2020.

This represents:

```text
Annual change in 2020: -18.05%
```

The recovery was not immediate:

| Year | Registrations | Annual change |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2020 | 256,949 | -18.05% |
| 2021 | 255,448 | -0.58% |
| 2022 | 270,732 | +5.98% |
| 2023 | 310,958 | +14.86% |
| 2024 | 316,880 | +1.90% |
| 2025 | 351,637 | +10.97% |

The recovery became clearer from 2022 onwards and culminated in the highest annual total of the series in 2025.

## 4. Transformation of propulsion technologies

The composition of the passenger car market changed substantially between 2015 and 2025.

| Propulsion technology | 2015 | 2025 | Change in percentage points |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Diesel | 74.74% | 10.56% | -64.18 |
| Petrol | 24.11% | 19.43% | -4.68 |
| Hybrid | 0.86% | 60.77% | +59.91 |
| Electric | 0.29% | 9.24% | +8.95 |

At the beginning of the period, diesel represented almost three quarters of all registrations.

By 2025:

- Diesel had fallen to 10.56%.
- Petrol represented 19.43%.
- Hybrid vehicles had become the dominant category, with 60.77%.
- Electric vehicles had increased to 9.24%.

The technological transition occurred primarily through hybrid vehicles. Battery-electric registrations also increased, although their final market share remained substantially below that of hybrid vehicles.

## 5. Vehicle power

The average power of registered passenger cars increased during the study period.

| Statistic | 2015 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean power | 90.53 kW | 100.94 kW |
| Median power | 88 kW | 100 kW |
| 90th percentile | 132 kW | 145 kW |

The results indicate that the technological transformation of the market was not accompanied by a reduction in vehicle performance.

The increase was particularly visible in the upper part of the power distribution.

## 6. Engine displacement

Average engine displacement declined between 2015 and 2025.

| Statistic | 2015 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean displacement | 1,656.46 cc | 1,557.11 cc |
| Median displacement | 1,598 cc | 1,498 cc |

The decline was concentrated primarily in the lower and middle parts of the distribution.

This pattern is consistent with a market combining smaller combustion engines with increasing levels of hybridisation and electrification.

## 7. CO2 emissions

The emissions series contains a methodological break associated with the transition from the NEDC measurement framework to WLTP.

Average reported emissions increased from:

```text
110.89 g/km in 2020
```

to:

```text
125.72 g/km in 2021
```

This increase should not be interpreted directly as a deterioration in environmental performance because the measurement procedure changed.

After 2021, average reported emissions followed a downward trend and reached:

```text
111.18 g/km in 2025
```

For the multivariate analysis, emissions were therefore standardised separately within the NEDC and WLTP periods.

## 8. Territorial concentration

Passenger car registrations are distributed very unevenly across municipalities.

The municipality with the highest number of registrations is Alcobendas:

```text
Alcobendas: 561,569 registrations
```

It is followed by Madrid city:

```text
Madrid city: 529,575 registrations
```

The concentration indicators show that:

| Ranking threshold | Cumulative share of registrations |
|---|---:|
| Largest municipality | 18.0% |
| Five largest municipalities | 55.3% |
| Ten largest municipalities | 74.9% |
| Twenty largest municipalities | 86.3% |

A very large proportion of registrations is therefore concentrated in a small number of municipalities.

## 9. Municipalities with atypical volumes

Several relatively small peripheral municipalities appear among those with the highest registration volumes.

Relevant examples include:

- Colmenar del Arroyo.
- Robledo de Chavela.
- Venturada.
- Brunete.
- Rozas de Puerto Real.
- Moralzarzal.

Their totals are too large to be interpreted solely as ordinary residential demand.

The results suggest that the municipality recorded in the data may reflect:

- Corporate registration strategies.
- Legal-entity ownership.
- Renting fleets.
- Administrative practices.
- Municipal taxation differences.

These explanations provide contextual interpretations, but the registration data alone do not establish a causal effect of municipal taxation.

## 10. Individual and legal ownership

Ownership patterns differ considerably between territorial groups.

| Territorial group | Individual ownership | Legal ownership |
|---|---:|---:|
| Madrid city | 81.4% | 18.6% |
| Remaining municipalities | 69.3% | 30.7% |
| Metropolitan or business municipalities | 27.8% | 72.2% |
| Atypical peripheral municipalities | 0.7% | 99.3% |

The atypical peripheral municipalities are almost entirely dominated by registrations associated with legal entities.

Examples include:

| Municipality | Total registrations | Legal-entity registrations |
|---|---:|---:|
| Alcobendas | 561,569 | 542,457 |
| Robledo de Chavela | 213,390 | 212,721 |
| Venturada | — | 156,883 |

These results reinforce the distinction between administrative registration location and actual residential use.

## 11. Renting concentration

Renting is strongly concentrated in the municipalities with the largest or most atypical registration volumes.

| Territorial group | Renting share |
|---|---:|
| Madrid city | 1.8% |
| Remaining municipalities | 4.7% |
| Metropolitan or business municipalities | 62.1% |
| Atypical peripheral municipalities | 79.2% |

Selected municipal results include:

| Municipality | Renting registrations | Total registrations | Approximate renting share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alcobendas | 490,585 | 561,569 | 87.4% |
| Colmenar del Arroyo | 236,888 | 260,362 | 91.0% |
| Robledo de Chavela | 189,499 | 213,390 | 88.8% |
| Brunete | 139,000 | 145,911 | 95.3% |

Renting and legal ownership explain a substantial part of the territorial concentration found in the data.

## 12. Temporal changes in municipal concentration

The leading municipalities do not follow identical trajectories.

Madrid city remains comparatively stable over much of the period, with a decline during the central years and a later recovery.

Alcobendas records strong growth from 2022 onwards and becomes the municipality with the highest annual registration volume at the end of the series.

Several atypical peripheral municipalities show more abrupt changes, reaching high volumes during specific years before losing relative importance later in the period.

This variability suggests that administrative registration strategies can change over time.

## 13. FAMD analytical sample

The Factor Analysis of Mixed Data was performed on a complete-case analytical sample containing:

```text
3,041,569 observations
```

This represents:

```text
97.64% of the final master dataset
```

The FAMD used eight active variables:

### Quantitative variables

- Engine displacement.
- Power in kilowatts.
- Normalised CO2 emissions.
- Electric energy consumption.
- Distance between axles.

### Qualitative variables

- Propulsion technology.
- Renting status.
- Individual or legal ownership.

## 15. Interpretation of the factorial dimensions

### Dimension 1: technology and environmental configuration

The first dimension is mainly associated with:

- Engine displacement.
- Normalised CO2 emissions.
- Propulsion technology.
- Electric energy consumption.

It separates vehicles according to their technological and environmental configuration.

### Dimension 2: size and performance

The second dimension is mainly associated with:

- Distance between axles.
- Vehicle power.
- Propulsion technology.
- Electric energy consumption.

It represents a combination of vehicle size, performance and technological configuration.

### Dimension 3: ownership and registration channel

The third dimension is dominated by:

- Renting status.
- Individual or legal ownership.

It distinguishes private registrations from corporate and renting-related registrations.

The FAMD therefore identifies three major sources of differentiation:

1. Technology and emissions.
2. Size and performance.
3. Ownership and registration channel.

## 16. Selection of the number of clusters

K-means clustering was applied to the factorial coordinates.

The mean silhouette values obtained were:

| Number of clusters | Mean silhouette |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.56 |
| 3 | 0.42 |
| 4 | 0.41 |
| 5 | 0.43 |
| 6 | 0.40 |
| 7 | 0.38 |

Although the two-cluster solution obtained the highest silhouette value, it was too aggregated to distinguish the relevant market profiles.

Among the more interpretable alternatives, the five-cluster solution obtained the best silhouette value.

The final choice balanced:

- Statistical separation.
- Stability.
- Parsimony.
- Interpretability.
- Ability to distinguish technological and ownership-related profiles.

## 17. Stability of the final cluster solution

The five-cluster solution produced identical results across the alternative random seeds tested.

The final model obtained:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Within-cluster sum of squares | 5,602,098 |
| Between-cluster sum of squares | 14,121,273 |
| Proportion of explained variation | 0.72 |

The solution was therefore considered stable and sufficiently informative for the objectives of the thesis.

## 18. Final cluster profiles

The final solution contains five differentiated market profiles.

| Cluster | Observations | Share | Proposed interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 165,153 | 5.4% | Business-oriented hybrid vehicles |
| 2 | 1,343,425 | 44.2% | Conventional company and renting vehicles |
| 3 | 482,263 | 15.9% | Higher-power and higher-displacement vehicles |
| 4 | 954,647 | 31.4% | Conventional privately owned vehicles |
| 5 | 96,081 | 3.2% | Electric vehicles |

The total number of clustered observations is 3,041,569.

## 19. Cluster 1: business-oriented hybrid vehicles

Cluster 1 is characterised by:

- 99.8% hybrid vehicles.
- 76.1% legal ownership.
- 59.3% renting.
- Mean power of 132.88 kW.
- Mean engine displacement of 1,791.74 cc.
- Mean electric consumption of 179.70 Wh/km.

This profile represents hybrid vehicles with a strong corporate and renting orientation.

## 20. Cluster 2: conventional company and renting vehicles

Cluster 2 is the largest group and represents 44.2% of the FAMD sample.

It is characterised by:

- A mixture of diesel, petrol and hybrid vehicles.
- 100% legal ownership.
- 87.8% renting.
- Mean power of 86.24 kW.
- Mean engine displacement of 1,494.95 cc.

This cluster represents the main corporate and renting channel.

## 21. Cluster 3: higher-power and higher-displacement vehicles

Cluster 3 is characterised by:

- 56.8% diesel vehicles.
- 35.3% hybrid vehicles.
- 85.6% legal ownership.
- 58.4% renting.
- Mean power of 143.17 kW.
- Mean engine displacement of 2,182.34 cc.
- The greatest average distance between axles among the five groups.

It represents larger and more powerful vehicles with a predominantly corporate orientation.

## 22. Cluster 4: conventional privately owned vehicles

Cluster 4 represents 31.4% of the analytical sample.

It is characterised by:

- 54.9% petrol vehicles.
- 82.8% individual ownership.
- 100% non-renting registrations.
- Mean power of 87.69 kW.
- Mean engine displacement of 1,423.94 cc.

This is the clearest conventional private-use profile.

## 23. Cluster 5: electric vehicles

Cluster 5 is characterised by:

- 99.9% electric vehicles.
- 62.1% legal ownership.
- 39.2% renting.
- Mean power of 147.58 kW.
- Mean engine displacement close to zero.
- Mean electric consumption of 161.58 Wh/km.

Although the cluster is predominantly associated with legal entities, it has a more mixed ownership and renting structure than the conventional corporate cluster.

## 24. Temporal evolution of the clusters

The cluster profiles do not remain constant throughout the study period.

The conventional private profile loses relative importance over time.

The corporate and renting profile remains central, particularly among legal entities.

The business-oriented hybrid cluster expands strongly as hybrid registrations increase.

The electric cluster also gains importance during the final years.

The temporal evolution confirms that the identified clusters represent structural changes in the composition of the market rather than only static statistical groups.

The STL decomposition separates the monthly series into:

The seasonal component remains sufficiently stable to confirm that the differences between months are not random.

The irregular component clearly identifies the exceptional disruption in 2020.

## 28. Main conclusions

The final results support the following conclusions:

1. Passenger car registrations increased substantially between 2015 and 2025.
2. The growth trend was interrupted by an exceptional decline in 2020.
3. Recovery became consolidated from 2022 onwards.
4. The market shifted from diesel dominance to hybrid dominance.
5. Electric registrations increased but remained below hybrid registrations.
6. Average vehicle power increased while average engine displacement declined.
7. Reported CO2 emissions require careful interpretation because of the transition from NEDC to WLTP.
8. Registrations are strongly concentrated in a small number of municipalities.
9. Legal entities and renting explain a substantial part of this territorial concentration.
10. The registration municipality does not necessarily represent the actual location of vehicle use.
11. FAMD identifies technological, performance-related and ownership-related dimensions.
12. Five differentiated market profiles summarise the heterogeneity of the registration market.
13. The monthly series presents a recurring seasonal structure, with lower activity in August and higher activity in December and early summer.

## 29. Interpretation limitations

The results must be interpreted within the following limitations:

- The dataset covers new registrations rather than the complete vehicle fleet.
- Administrative registration location may differ from actual vehicle use.
- Corporate fleets can substantially distort municipal totals.
- Fiscal explanations are contextual hypotheses rather than causal results derived directly from the dataset.
- Technical variables contain missing and non-applicable values.
- The transition from NEDC to WLTP introduces a methodological break.
- The FAMD analytical sample uses complete observations for the selected variables.
- Cluster results depend on the variables, preprocessing decisions and retained factorial dimensions.
- The statistical analyses identify associations and profiles, not causal effects.
